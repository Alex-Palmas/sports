    Arbitrage & middles

    Kalshi Latency Study

    Follows every NBA and NFL game live against every Kalshi market for that game. Compare how each market type moves and how fast it reprices after scores, and see whether a free feed sees plays before Kalshi does. For every play that settles a player prop we follow the price for two minutes: when it starts moving, how far it goes, when it's halfway and 90% done, and how much of the move was left when the feed delivered the play. Read-only research: nothing here places orders.

    Prop latency

    How it's measured
    • Play time is the wall-clock stamp the feed attaches to each play (ESPN's wallclock, the NBA feed's timeActual).
    • Feed delay is when our poller (about once a second, from a GitHub server) first saw the play, minus the play time.
    • Kalshi adjusts when the YES bid on the settled market first reaches 90¢ or Kalshi closes the market. Kalshi's own update stamp is used when it falls between two polls; otherwise the poll time.
    • Stale price is the YES ask Kalshi still showed when the feed delivered the play, and the profit per contract if bought then and settled at $1, after Kalshi's fee. It ignores how long your order takes to arrive and how many contracts were offered.
    • Price path: Kalshi's implied probability (the middle of the best bid and ask) at fixed moments from 10 seconds before the play to 2 minutes after. For a prop the play settled, the end point is 100¢. For the game line it is the price just before the next score, or 2 minutes later, so the next basket isn't counted as this one's move.
    • Starts / 50% / 90% done: seconds after the play until the price first changed, and until it covered half and 90% of the total move. Moves under 1 point of probability count as no move.
    • Price steps and contracts traded: how many separate price changes the move took, and how many contracts changed hands meanwhile. Many small steps with steady trading mean the price drifts as people trade. A single jump means market makers repriced at once.
    • Left when feed showed it: the share of the move still to come when our feed delivered the play. This is the part a trader on this feed could still capture, before fees and order delay.
    • Game tracker: each game's Kalshi markets are found from its moneyline event, then polled by market type within Kalshi's public rate limit (about 4 requests a second). Moneyline, spread, total and the headline prop refresh every few seconds; other props and quarter/half markets refresh less often, and each type's refresh interval is shown. Price changes are timed with Kalshi's own update stamp when it falls between two polls.
    • Game line by situation splits scores into the last 5 minutes of a game within 8 points ("clutch") and the rest, and by 1, 2 and 3 points.
    • Polling once a second adds up to about a second of error to each timing. Plays that were already priced at 90¢ or more beforehand are excluded.

    Crypto Bot

    A paper-trading bot on Kalshi's Bitcoin and Ethereum markets (hourly above/below, hourly ranges, 15-minute up/down). It prices every market from the live exchange price, compares that with Kalshi's odds, and paper-trades when Kalshi lags. Two strategies run side by side: Latency uses Kalshi's own volatility and only wins if it reacts to price moves before Kalshi does; Model uses our volatility estimate. Positions are held to Kalshi's actual result. No orders are ever sent.
